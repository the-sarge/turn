## Goal

Make one binding-local readiness module own readiness facts, legal transitions, confirmation age, prepared history, stored failure, and terminal collapse, so `UDPConn` asks outcome-shaped questions instead of reading and mutating raw state. Preserve `UDPConn` as the sole owner of TURN transaction policy, worker accounting, and Allocation lifecycle.

## Current Shape (verified 2026-08-19 at `122019cf5a040bcb7a8ba9002bd3a82e9ad947cf`)

`binding` stores seven raw states, a prepared atomic, refreshed timestamp, last error, terminal flag, attempt channel, and two locks, and exposes state/time/error getters and setters plus `ok` and `terminalize` (`internal/client/binding.go:23-110`). `bindingManager` separately owns immutable peer/channel identity, capacity, and the address-to-channel bijection (`internal/client/binding.go:112-193`).

`UDPConn` interprets readiness in multiple places. Prepare checks `bindingResult`, duplicates ten-minute expiry, marks `prepared`, and reads raw failure (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:300-369`). Worker start reads and writes raw state to choose initial versus refresh work (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:371-398`, `internal/client/udp_conn.go:660-685`). Attempt completion records error separately and later writes confirmation time/state (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:385-395`, `internal/client/udp_conn.go:688-720`). Transaction failures, fresh/previously-ready 400 policy, and permanent failure directly choose raw successor states (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:722-765`). `WriteTo` independently checks prepared, repeats the same expiry rule, reads readiness/error, and sends the channel (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:425-455`). Permission-refresh failure iterates bindings and terminalizes only those whose prepared atomic is set (`internal/client/udp_conn.go:414-423`).

Tests frequently manufacture and inspect private state, timestamps, errors, and prepared flags rather than enter through readiness behavior (`internal/client/udp_conn_test.go:319-331`, `internal/client/udp_conn_test.go:711-775`; `internal/client/prepare_test.go:288-298`, `internal/client/prepare_test.go:568-579`, `internal/client/prepare_test.go:623-633`). The finite transitions are therefore split between storage, orchestration, and tests.

Existing behavior distinguishes channel-binding identity from Permission identity, refresh eligibility from ten-minute expiry, fresh from previously confirmed attempts, prepared history from current usability, transaction uncertainty from permanent failure, and binding terminality from Allocation seal. Those distinctions remain normative.

## Decision

`binding` owns all readiness storage and transition mechanics behind outcome-shaped operations. Raw state labels, timestamp/error setters, prepared flag mutation, `ok`, and terminal-collapse mechanics become private implementation details of `binding.go`; callers cannot inspect or manufacture them. `bindingManager` remains the channel-number capacity and peer-transport-address identity owner and does not absorb readiness.

The readiness seam supports four concrete semantic behaviors without exposing a generic event engine: `beginAttempt(now, refreshAfter)` returns an identity token, a fresh-versus-previously-confirmed attempt class, and whether work should start; `resolveAttempt(token, semanticOutcome, now)` applies one attempt result; separate preparation/write access decisions accept `now`; and `failPrepared(cause)` applies tokenless permission-refresh loss. Exact private Go spelling may vary, but these are the complete operations: there is no public event union, purpose flag, raw state access, or callback. The attempt token owns generation identity and can resolve at most once; a stale, duplicate, or late token cannot overwrite a terminal binding or a newer attempt. The attempt class is semantic rather than a raw state and is the only prior-readiness fact exposed to `UDPConn`. `failPrepared` serializes on the readiness lock, does nothing before prepared history, terminalizes with the supplied `ErrPermissionRefreshFailed` cause after preparation, preserves the first terminal cause, and never seals the Allocation.

`UDPConn` continues to build and execute ChannelBind, retry stale nonce, classify transport/transaction failure, identify 400 versus other TURN failures, register and join workers, coordinate one shared attempt, and own `startClose`. For 400 it uses only the fresh-versus-previously-confirmed attempt class: previously confirmed selects preservation, while fresh selects permanent failure and Allocation seal. It passes only the already-selected attempt result to `resolveAttempt`: confirmed now, transaction outcome uncertain, preserve prior confirmation, or permanent failure with its cause. Permission refresh calls `failPrepared` separately because loss is not an attempt completion and carries no attempt token. Readiness applies the legal transition and returns stable status needed by orchestration; it never performs network I/O, worker registration, retries, error-code classification, or Allocation sealing.

Attempt coordination and durable readiness have separate result ownership. One active attempt object under `muBind` owns its done signal and that generation's transient result, publishes the result before closing the signal, and surfaces it only to waiters that joined that attempt. Binding readiness owns only a durable terminal cause. Fresh transaction uncertainty moves readiness to retryable unknown while current waiters receive their attempt error; a later `PreparePeer` may start another attempt on the same channel. Previously confirmed uncertainty preserves usable history and makes refresh eligible while current attempt waiters receive the attempt error only where existing orchestration surfaces it. Permanent failure atomically records its specific cause with terminal readiness. Exhausted 438 remains `errTryAgain` without a typed TURN error; malformed and typed permanent errors preserve their existing chains. No error is stored in both attempt coordination and durable readiness.

Preparation marks the peer prepared only when a `PreparePeer` waiter invokes preparation access and observes a confirmed, unexpired binding. Server confirmation alone never marks prepared. If cancellation or close is selected before preparation access, prepared history is not established; if completed readiness is selected and observed first, preparation may return success even when cancellation becomes ready concurrently. Shared work is never canceled by one waiter. Write access requires prepared history and current unexpired usability. Expiry at or beyond the ten-minute binding lifetime terminalizes once with `ErrChannelBindingExpired`; a later in-flight completion cannot resurrect it. Refresh eligibility remains strictly after the configured refresh interval. A transport/transaction failure from a fresh binding becomes retryable unknown, while the same failure after confirmation preserves ready history as uncertain. A recovered previously confirmed 400 does not advance confirmation time. Permanent failures retain their specific cause. Permission-refresh loss and preparation linearize on the same binding readiness lock: loss before prepared history does not terminalize, while loss after preparation terminalizes with `ErrPermissionRefreshFailed` and does not seal the Allocation.

Every time-dependent readiness decision receives `time.Time` from the immediate caller on one nondecreasing logical timeline with a positive refresh interval. A new binding begins with no confirmation time; `bindingManager` no longer manufactures a readiness timestamp. Refresh is eligible only when `now.Sub(confirmedAt) > refreshInterval`; expiry occurs when `now.Sub(confirmedAt) >= channelBindingLifetime`; a recovered previously confirmed 400 does not change `confirmedAt`. Reordered or arbitrary external timestamps are outside the supported domain. No clock interface, fake clock, Allocation-wide scheduler, or private timestamp backdating is introduced. Attempt joining and its active attempt object remain outside readiness for this slice and are re-audited only at the program checkpoint after merge.

**Rejected alternative (do not do this):** Move raw accessors into another file or rename the enum without changing callers. That preserves the shallow state protocol.

**Rejected alternative (do not do this):** Introduce a generic event/state-machine framework, public event union, or callback-driven prepared-peer object. The supported binding transition table is local and finite; generic machinery would enlarge the interface and split ownership.

**Rejected alternative (do not do this):** Move ChannelBind execution, nonce/retry policy, 400 classification, Permission orchestration, worker accounting, attempt coalescing, or Allocation seal/join into binding readiness.

**Non-goals:** No channel-number capacity, binding reclamation, peer identity, TURN bytes, retry count, refresh cadence, permission lifecycle, Allocation lifecycle, public API, broad clock, timer, attempt-coalescing, responder-harness, or package-layout change.

## Implementation Slices

### Slice T3.S1 — Make binding readiness the transition owner

**What it delivers:** One PR introducing outcome-shaped binding-local decisions, migrating PreparePeer, scheduled refresh, ChannelBind completion/error disposition, prepared-only WriteTo, and permission-loss terminalization to them, deleting outward raw readiness mechanics, and replacing private-state-poking tests with a finite readiness table plus preserved Allocation-level behavior and race evidence.

**Implementation:** PR #76 is the slice's one intended product PR.

**Existing-work disposition:** New slice. There are no open issues, PRs, or implementation branches to retain or rebaseline as of 2026-08-19.

**Blocked by:** None.

**Single owner after merge:** `binding` readiness is the only mutation owner for readiness phase, prior-confirmation history, confirmation time, prepared history, durable terminal cause, expiry, and binding terminality. The `UDPConn` attempt-coordination path is the only owner of each active attempt's identity, done signal, and transient result; no error is stored in both places. `bindingManager` alone owns channel identity/capacity and constructs readiness with no confirmation. `UDPConn` alone owns worker accounting, TURN classification, Permission orchestration, and Allocation seal/release/join.

**Authority completeness:** No persisted fact. The same slice covers binding construction, every readiness producer (foreground/scheduled attempt, success, transaction uncertainty, 400 recovery/failure, other permanent failure, permission loss, expiry), both destructive consumers (PreparePeer and WriteTo), and all direct mutation paths. No raw generic setter remains.

**Transitional-seam budget:** None at merge. Outcome-shaped operations and old raw access may coexist only during branch migration. The intended PR cannot retain raw readiness mutation in `UDPConn` or behavior tests, a second expiry calculation, a second prepared marker, or shared storage for transient attempt result and durable terminal cause. Retained `muBind` and the active attempt object are established coordination bookkeeping, not an alternate readiness authority.

**Blast radius:** Concurrent PreparePeer callers, scheduled binding refresh, ChannelBind retry/error recovery, transient-versus-durable error ownership, permission-refresh failure, WriteTo admission, ten-minute expiry, close races, error chains, and internal tests. One binding's readiness operations serialize on its readiness lock without serializing different bindings. No readiness lock is held across TURN I/O, worker registration, waiting, Allocation seal, or caller callbacks; readiness and `muBind` are never nested in opposing orders. `WriteTo` access remains bounded to in-process state and emits zero network output on denial. Immutable peer/channel identity, manager capacity, exact TURN and ChannelData bytes, retry counts, refresh/check intervals, worker lifetime, waiter-local cancellation, Allocation terminal cause, and public interfaces are preserved. No benchmark or general performance guarantee is part of the slice. Untraced effects are not accepted: any raw caller transition, second expiry rule, lifecycle move, global binding serialization, lock held across I/O, reverse lock order, or need for a broad clock stops the slice.

**Artifact classification:** Readiness decisions and terminal-collapse guards are shipped behavior and required lifecycle enforcement. Finite transition tables, focused PreparePeer/WriteTo tests, existing wire tests, and race tests are verification aids. Plans, issues, receipts, and tasks are process or traceability metadata. No verification aid becomes a maintained product deliverable.

**Representation contract:** Supported domain = one binding's finite readiness history on a nondecreasing logical `time.Time` timeline with positive refresh interval and ten-minute lifetime; initial/refresh eligibility; active fresh/previously-confirmed attempt; successful confirmation; fresh/previously-confirmed transaction uncertainty; fresh/previously-confirmed 400 disposition selected by `UDPConn`; permanent failure; permission loss before/after preparation; waiter observation/cancellation selection; preparation/write access; Allocation close; terminal state; and duplicate/stale/late completion. Reordered external timestamps and arbitrary scheduler interleavings outside the semantic order classes below are non-goals. Root canonicalization and `bindingManager` own immutable peer/channel representation; readiness owns the finite state, token validity, and semantic attempt class; attempt coordination owns per-generation done/result; `UDPConn` owns semantic classification of TURN results and Allocation close. Guarantee = universal over the finite semantic matrix below, not over arbitrary server behavior or protocol syntax. Terminating evidence = one table row per behaviorally distinct transition/access/ordering class, exact threshold cases, one required terminal-guard mutation, focused public behavior, existing wire/lifecycle tests, `go test -race ./...`, `task preflight`, same-head hosted CI, and one review plus at most one replacement.

**Contract closure:** Triggered because an invalid terminal or prepared transition can resurrect failed writes, emit unprepared ChannelData, mask permission loss, or corrupt lifecycle behavior, and readiness is reached independently through foreground preparation, scheduled refresh, write access, Permission failure, server 400, transport failure, expiry, cancellation, and close.

**Evidence budget:** The matrix rows are the complete semantic budget. Use explicit values from one nondecreasing `time.Time` timeline for refresh just below/at/above its strict boundary and lifetime just below/at/above its inclusive expiry boundary; one representative error per semantic failure class; controlled fresh/refresh in-flight access; deterministic cancellation-selected then second-caller observation; both permission-loss/preparation orders; both permanent-outcome/access orders; expiry/completion order; Allocation close without readiness mutation; token duplicate/stale/late rejection; existing end-to-end fresh/ready 400, permission-refresh, prepared-only write, request-byte, and close tests; and `go test -race ./...`. Require one discriminating mutation: bypass terminal collapse and demonstrate that the late-resolution/non-resurrection test fails. No fake clock, repeated sleeps, fuzzing, exhaustive scheduler matrix, new platform cells, generic harness, or recursive mutation. Termination = every matrix row covered/rejected as stated, the required mutation, full gates, one fresh review, at most one replacement, and no `stop-for-decision` finding.

**TDD and preservation evidence:** Write the binding-local begin/resolve/preparation/write table first, including token generation and transient-versus-durable result ownership. Add explicit-time threshold and controlled ordering tests before migrating production callers. Then migrate PreparePeer and WriteTo while retaining their public behavior tables, followed by scheduled refresh and error/lifecycle flows. Drive the cancellation-selected branch deterministically without adding cancellation precedence. Delete raw-state test setup only after equivalent behavior evidence exists. Preserve exact request shapes and race-clean worker/close behavior.

**Dispatch context budget:** This slice contract; `internal/client/binding.go`; `internal/client/udp_conn.go:187-455,660-765`; focused binding/UDPConn/PreparePeer tests; the prepared-only, TURN consistency, and Allocation lifecycle plans. Treat attempt-coalescing and construction checkpoints as non-goals. No root inbound or transaction implementation and no historical review transcript is required. The change is one finite in-process state owner, five production consumers, and bounded tests and fits one fresh context, but any new lifecycle owner or generic framework exceeds the budget.

**Slice decision audit:** Strongest further split = centralize readiness transitions first and remove raw access/tests in a second PR. Rejected because the first would leave a generic mutation path and dual semantic owners, violating the single-owner and transitional-seam gates. Strongest merge = combine attempt coalescing because `attemptDone` sits on binding. Rejected because coalescing has materially different Permission semantics and would add lifecycle/concurrency scope before the readiness result is known. No cross-track blocker is required; outbound transport and inbound delivery consume separate seams.

**Stop conditions:** `UDPConn` must still inspect or mutate raw readiness beyond the fresh/previously-confirmed attempt class; transient attempt results and durable causes cannot have separate owners; outcome operations need request setters, nonce/retry policy, worker callbacks, or Allocation lifecycle actions; a generic event/state framework or broad clock becomes necessary; caller cancellation cannot remain waiter-local without adding precedence; old-ready 400 preservation requires advancing confirmation time; exact thresholds or error chains must change; per-binding locking requires global serialization, I/O under lock, or reverse lock order; or the one-PR migration cannot remove every alternate readiness mutation path.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Every binding readiness fact and transition in the supported finite domain has one owner in `binding`; representation owner, universal guarantee, matrix, and terminating evidence are defined in T3.S1.
- [x] `UDPConn` retains TURN classification, 400 disposition, attempt coordination, worker accounting, Permission orchestration, and Allocation seal/release/join without reading or writing raw binding state.
- [x] Preparation establishes prepared history only when a waiter invokes preparation access and observes confirmed unexpired readiness; cancellation/close selection remains waiter-local without new precedence, and writes remain prepared-only with zero output after expiry or terminal failure.
- [x] Attempt coordination alone owns each joined attempt's transient result; readiness alone owns a durable terminal cause; later retry after fresh uncertainty and all existing error chains preserve the declared matrix.
- [x] Refresh eligibility, ten-minute expiry, in-flight access, fresh/ready uncertainty, fresh/ready 400 behavior, permission-loss ordering, close behavior, token validity, specific failure causes, and terminal non-resurrection preserve the declared matrix.
- [x] The complete mutation seam is begin/resolve attempt, preparation/write access, and tokenless prepared-permission failure; no raw readiness getter/setter, direct terminal mutation, duplicate expiry calculation, second prepared marker, generic state engine, broad clock, prepared-peer module, or attempt-coalescing abstraction remains or is introduced.
- [x] New bindings begin with no confirmation time, and channel identity/capacity, exact wire bytes, retry counts, intervals, public API, caller cancellation, and Allocation lifecycle remain unchanged.
